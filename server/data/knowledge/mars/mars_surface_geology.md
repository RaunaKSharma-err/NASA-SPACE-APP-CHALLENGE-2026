# Mars Surface and Geology

## Major Surface Features
- **Impact craters:** Common across Mars, especially in older highland regions.
- **Volcanoes:** Mars hosts the largest known volcano in the Solar System, Olympus Mons.
- **Canyons:** Valles Marineris is a vast canyon system stretching thousands of kilometers.
- **Plains and basins:** Large lowland plains and impact basins shape the planet’s topography.
- **Polar regions:** Ice caps composed of water ice and seasonal carbon dioxide ice.

## Geological History (high level)
- Mars preserves evidence of ancient geological processes, including volcanism, impacts, erosion, and sedimentation.
- Some regions show layered rocks and minerals that formed in the presence of water in the past.
- Current Mars is cold and dry at the surface, but geological records indicate a more active and potentially wetter past.

## Rocks and Minerals
- Orbital and rover observations have identified basaltic rocks, sedimentary deposits, clays, sulfates, and other minerals.
- Mineral types can provide clues about past environmental conditions (e.g., neutral vs acidic water).

## Relevance to Exploration
- Terrain type, slopes, rocks, and dust affect rover and human mobility.
- Geological context helps identify scientifically interesting sites and potential past habitable environments.

## Limitations
- Surface composition and geology vary greatly by region.
- Remote sensing and rover findings are site-specific and should not be generalized to all of Mars.
