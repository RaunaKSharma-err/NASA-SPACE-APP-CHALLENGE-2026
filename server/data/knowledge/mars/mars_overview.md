# Mars Overview

## Summary
Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun and a rocky (terrestrial) planet. It is often called the Red Planet because iron oxide (rust) in its surface materials gives it a reddish appearance.

## Key Facts (approximate)
- **Planet type:** Terrestrial (rocky)
- **Order from Sun:** 4th
- **Equatorial diameter:** ~6,779 km
- **Mean radius:** ~3,390 km
- **Mass:** ~6.42 × 10^23 kg (about 10.7% of Earth’s mass)
- **Surface gravity:** ~3.71 m/s² (about 38% of Earth’s)
- **Mean density:** ~3.93 g/cm³
- **Length of day (sol, solar day):** ~24 hours 39 minutes 35 seconds (sidereal rotation period ~24 hours 37 minutes 22 seconds)
- **Length of year:** ~687 Earth days
- **Natural satellites:** 2 (Phobos and Deimos)

## Environment at a Glance
- **Atmosphere:** Thin, dominated by carbon dioxide (~95%)
- **Average surface pressure:** ~610 Pa (~0.6% of Earth’s sea-level pressure)
- **Surface temperatures:** Highly variable; can range roughly from about -125 °C near poles in winter to about 20 °C near equator in summer (conditions vary by location, season, and time of day)
- **Surface:** Impact craters, volcanoes, canyons, plains, polar ice caps, and evidence of ancient water-related processes

## Why Mars Matters for Exploration
Mars is a major target for robotic and potential future human exploration because it preserves geological records of early planetary history, shows evidence that liquid water existed in the past, and presents environmental challenges relevant to long-duration missions.

## Limitations
- Values are approximate and can vary by measurement method and reference model.
- Surface conditions vary significantly by location, altitude, season, and local weather.
