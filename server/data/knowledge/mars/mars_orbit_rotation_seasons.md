# Mars Orbit, Rotation, and Seasons

## Rotation (Length of Day)
- **Sidereal day:** ~24 hours 37 minutes 22 seconds
- **Solar day (sol):** ~24 hours 39 minutes 35 seconds
- A Martian day is slightly longer than an Earth day.

## Orbit (Length of Year)
- **Orbital period:** ~687 Earth days (~1.88 Earth years)
- **Average distance from Sun:** ~227.9 million km (~1.52 AU)
- **Orbital eccentricity:** ~0.093 (more elliptical than Earth’s orbit)

## Axial Tilt and Seasons
- **Axial tilt:** ~25.2° (similar to Earth’s ~23.4°)
- Because of its axial tilt, Mars experiences seasons.
- Mars’ more elliptical orbit makes seasons vary in length and intensity between hemispheres.

## Implications for Missions
- Day/night cycles affect solar power availability and surface operations.
- Seasonal changes influence temperature, dust activity, and polar ice behavior.
- Mission planners must account for sol-based scheduling rather than Earth-day scheduling.

## Limitations
- Seasonal effects vary by latitude and local terrain.
- Dust storms and weather can alter surface conditions significantly.
