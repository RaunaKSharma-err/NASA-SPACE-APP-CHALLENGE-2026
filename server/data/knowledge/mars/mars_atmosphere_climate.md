# Mars Atmosphere and Climate

## Atmospheric Composition (by volume, approximate)
- **Carbon dioxide (CO2):** ~95%
- **Nitrogen (N2):** ~2.8%
- **Argon (Ar):** ~2%
- **Oxygen (O2):** ~0.13%
- **Carbon monoxide (CO):** ~0.08%
- Trace amounts of water vapor and other gases

## Pressure and Density
- **Average surface pressure:** ~610 Pa (about 0.006 bar), roughly 0.6% of Earth’s sea-level pressure
- Pressure varies with altitude and season.
- The thin atmosphere provides little insulation and limited protection from radiation.

## Temperature
- Mars is generally cold due to its thin atmosphere and distance from the Sun.
- Temperatures vary widely by location, season, time of day, and elevation.
- Typical ranges can span from very cold polar/winter conditions (below -100 °C) to near 20 °C in some daytime equatorial conditions.

## Weather and Dust
- Mars experiences winds, clouds, frost, and dust activity.
- **Dust devils** are common local phenomena.
- **Regional and global dust storms** can occur, reducing visibility and sunlight for solar-powered systems.

## Habitability Implications
- Humans cannot breathe the Martian atmosphere.
- Low pressure means unprotected humans cannot survive on the surface.
- Dust and temperature extremes are key engineering and safety challenges.

## Limitations
- Atmospheric values are averages; local conditions vary.
- Dust storm timing and intensity are not fully predictable.
