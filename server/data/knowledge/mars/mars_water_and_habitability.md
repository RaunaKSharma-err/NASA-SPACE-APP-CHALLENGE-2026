# Mars Water and Habitability

## Evidence of Past Water
Multiple lines of evidence indicate that liquid water existed on ancient Mars:
- **Valley networks and channels:** Suggest past surface water flow in some regions.
- **Delta and lake deposits:** Sedimentary structures consistent with standing water and river deltas.
- **Hydrated minerals:** Clays and sulfates detected from orbit and by rovers indicate past water-related alteration.
- **Rover observations:** Sedimentary rocks and mineral assemblages at sites like Gale Crater and Jezero Crater support past aqueous environments.

## Present-Day Water
- **Polar ice caps:** Contain water ice (and seasonal CO2 ice).
- **Subsurface ice:** Evidence suggests ice exists below the surface in many regions, especially at higher latitudes.
- Liquid water is not stable on most of the surface today due to low pressure and temperature, though transient brines are a topic of scientific study and debate.

## Habitability (past and present)
- **Past habitability:** Some ancient environments on Mars may have been habitable for microbial life, based on evidence of water, energy sources, and suitable chemistry.
- **Current habitability:** The surface is harsh (cold, thin atmosphere, radiation). No confirmed present-day life has been detected.
- “Habitable” means conditions could support life; it does not mean life existed.

## Limitations and Uncertainty
- Evidence strength varies by location and method.
- Interpretations of some features can be debated; claims should distinguish observation from inference.
- Detecting biosignatures is challenging and requires careful analysis.
