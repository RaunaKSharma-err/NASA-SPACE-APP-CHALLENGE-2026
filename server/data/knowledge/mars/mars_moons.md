# Mars Moons: Phobos and Deimos

## Overview
Mars has two small, irregularly shaped moons: **Phobos** and **Deimos**. They are much smaller than Earth’s Moon and have low surface gravity.

## Phobos
- **Mean radius:** ~11.1 km (irregular shape)
- **Orbit:** Very close to Mars; orbits faster than Mars rotates
- **Orbital period:** ~7 hours 39 minutes
- **Surface:** Heavily cratered; includes the large Stickney crater
- **Notable fact:** Phobos is gradually spiraling inward and may break apart or impact Mars far in the future (long-term prediction)

## Deimos
- **Mean radius:** ~6.2 km (irregular shape)
- **Orbit:** Farther from Mars than Phobos
- **Orbital period:** ~30 hours 18 minutes
- **Surface:** Cratered but appears smoother in some areas due to regolith coverage

## Origin (scientific hypotheses)
- Possible origins include captured asteroids or formation from debris after an impact on Mars.
- The exact origin remains an area of scientific study.

## Limitations
- Sizes and orbital values are approximate reference figures.
- Origin theories are hypotheses, not confirmed facts.
