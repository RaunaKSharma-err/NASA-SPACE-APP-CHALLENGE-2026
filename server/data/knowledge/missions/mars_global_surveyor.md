# Mars Global Surveyor (MGS)

## Mission Purpose
Mars Global Surveyor (MGS) was a NASA orbiter mission designed to study the entire Martian surface, atmosphere, and interior, and to provide high-resolution mapping and long-term monitoring.

## Key Dates (selected)
- **Launch:** November 7, 1996
- **Mars orbit insertion:** September 11, 1997
- **Primary mapping mission:** 1999–2001 (with extended operations afterward)
- **Last contact:** November 2, 2006

## Major Instruments (selected)
- **MOLA (Mars Orbiter Laser Altimeter):** Measured surface topography/elevation globally.
- **MOC (Mars Orbiter Camera):** Imaged surface features and atmospheric phenomena.
- **TES (Thermal Emission Spectrometer):** Studied surface composition and atmospheric properties.
- **MAG/ER (Magnetometer/Electron Reflectometer):** Measured magnetic fields and helped reveal crustal magnetism.

## Major Contributions
- Produced detailed global topographic maps of Mars (MOLA).
- Improved understanding of Martian geology, climate, and surface processes.
- Provided evidence relevant to past water activity and crustal magnetization.
- Served as a communications relay for other missions during parts of its operation.

## Data Types
- Topography/elevation data, imagery, thermal and spectral observations, magnetic field data.

## Limitations
- Historical mission; status and datasets should be referenced as mission-era products.
- Instrument coverage, resolution, and processing vary by product.
