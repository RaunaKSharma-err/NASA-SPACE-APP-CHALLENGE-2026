# Mars 2020 (Perseverance Rover)

## Mission Purpose
Perseverance explores Jezero Crater to study its geology, seek signs of ancient microbial life, collect and cache samples for possible future return to Earth, and test technologies for future human exploration.

## Key Dates (selected)
- **Launch:** July 30, 2020
- **Landing:** February 18, 2021 in Jezero Crater
- **Mission:** Ongoing rover operations (refer to current mission updates for latest status)

## Landing Site
- **Jezero Crater:** Site of an ancient river delta and lake system, selected for its potential to preserve biosignatures.

## Major Instruments (selected)
- **Mastcam-Z:** Zoomable stereo imaging.
- **SuperCam:** Remote spectroscopy, imaging, and microphone.
- **PIXL:** High-resolution chemical mapping.
- **SHERLOC:** Organic and mineral detection using fluorescence/Raman techniques.
- **RIMFAX:** Ground-penetrating radar.
- **MOXIE:** Technology demo producing oxygen from Martian CO2 (completed its planned demonstrations).

## Major Contributions
- Investigated ancient delta and crater-floor rocks in Jezero Crater.
- Collected and cached rock/soil samples for potential Mars Sample Return.
- Demonstrated Ingenuity helicopter technology flights (separate technology demonstration).
- Conducted in situ analyses relevant to past aqueous environments and astrobiology.

## Data Types
- High-resolution imagery, spectroscopy, subsurface radar, chemical maps, cached samples (physical).

## Limitations
- Results are specific to Jezero Crater; not directly applicable to Gale Crater.
- Biosignature investigations are complex; no confirmed past or present life announced.
