# Mars Science Laboratory (Curiosity Rover)

## Mission Purpose
Curiosity is a car-sized rover sent to Gale Crater to investigate Mars’ climate and geology and assess whether ancient environments could have supported microbial life.

## Key Dates (selected)
- **Launch:** November 26, 2011
- **Landing:** August 6, 2012 (UTC) at Bradbury Landing in Gale Crater
- **Mission:** Ongoing rover operations (status varies over time; refer to current mission updates for latest status)

## Landing Site
- **Gale Crater:** ~154 km diameter impact crater with a central mound, Aeolis Mons (Mount Sharp).

## Major Instruments (selected)
- **Mastcam:** Mast-mounted cameras for imaging and color context.
- **ChemCam:** Laser-induced breakdown spectroscopy (LIBS) and remote micro-imager for chemistry/targets at distance.
- **SAM (Sample Analysis at Mars):** Analyzes gases and organic compounds in atmosphere and drilled/soil samples.
- **CheMin:** X-ray diffraction for mineral identification in powdered samples.
- **REMS:** Weather and environmental monitoring.
- **RAD:** Radiation assessment detector.

## Major Scientific Contributions
- Found evidence that ancient Gale Crater had environments with liquid water and conditions that could have been habitable for microbial life in the past.
- Characterized sedimentary rocks and mineral transitions in Mount Sharp’s layers.
- Measured radiation environment relevant to future human exploration.
- Detected organic molecules in ancient rocks (detection of organics does not equal detection of life).

## Data Types
- In situ images, chemical/mineralogical analyses, environmental and radiation measurements.

## Limitations
- Findings are specific to Gale Crater and traversed locations.
- “Habitable past environment” means conditions could support life; no confirmed life detected.
