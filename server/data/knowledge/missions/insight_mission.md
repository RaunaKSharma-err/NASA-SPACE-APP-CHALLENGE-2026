# InSight (Interior Exploration using Seismic Investigations, Geodesy and Heat Transport)

## Mission Purpose
NASA’s InSight lander studied the deep interior of Mars, including its crust, mantle, and core, to improve understanding of rocky planet formation and evolution.

## Key Dates (selected)
- **Launch:** May 5, 2018
- **Landing:** November 26, 2018 at Elysium Planitia
- **End of mission:** Declared end of mission in December 2022 after power loss due to dust accumulation

## Major Instruments (selected)
- **SEIS (Seismic Experiment for Interior Structure):** Detected marsquakes and seismic waves.
- **HP3 (Heat Flow and Physical Properties Package):** Mole instrument intended to measure heat flow (encountered deployment challenges).
- **RISE (Rotation and Interior Structure Experiment):** Tracked radio signals to study planetary rotation and core properties.
- **Weather sensors and cameras:** Monitored local environment.

## Major Contributions
- First mission dedicated primarily to studying Mars’ interior.
- Detected and characterized marsquakes, providing constraints on crust, mantle, and core structure.
- Improved estimates of core size and properties and advanced understanding of Martian seismicity.

## Data Types
- Seismic waveforms, radio tracking data, atmospheric/weather measurements.

## Limitations
- Single-station seismic measurements limit localization precision compared to a network.
- Heat-flow objectives were partially impacted by deployment issues.
