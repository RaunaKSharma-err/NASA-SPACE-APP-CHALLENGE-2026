# Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (MRO)

## Mission Purpose
NASA’s Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter studies Mars’ climate, geology, and potential landing sites, and serves as a key data relay for surface missions.

## Key Dates (selected)
- **Launch:** August 12, 2005
- **Mars orbit insertion:** March 10, 2006
- **Primary science phase:** Began 2006 after aerobraking; mission extended multiple times

## Major Instruments (selected)
- **HiRISE (High Resolution Imaging Science Experiment):** Very high-resolution surface imaging.
- **CRISM (Compact Reconnaissance Imaging Spectrometer for Mars):** Visible/near-infrared spectral data for mineral identification.
- **CTX (Context Camera):** Wide-area context imaging.
- **SHARAD (Shallow Radar):** Subsurface radar sounding.
- **MCS (Mars Climate Sounder):** Atmospheric temperature, dust, and ice profiles.

## Major Contributions
- Detailed imaging of surface features, landing sites, and seasonal changes.
- Detection and mapping of minerals associated with past water (e.g., clays, sulfates).
- Subsurface radar observations of ice-rich deposits in some regions.
- Long-term climate and atmospheric monitoring.
- Critical communications relay support for rovers and landers.

## Data Types
- High-resolution imagery, hyperspectral mineral data, radar soundings, atmospheric profiles.

## Limitations
- Orbital remote sensing provides indirect measurements; ground truth requires landers/rovers.
- Coverage/resolution vary by instrument and target.
