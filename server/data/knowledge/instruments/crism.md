# CRISM (Compact Reconnaissance Imaging Spectrometer for Mars)

## Overview
CRISM is a visible and near-infrared imaging spectrometer on NASA’s Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (MRO). It is used to identify and map minerals on the Martian surface.

## Mission and Platform
- **Instrument:** CRISM
- **Spacecraft:** Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (MRO)

## What It Measures
- **Data type:** Spectral data in visible to infrared wavelengths
- **Purpose:** Detect mineral signatures associated with past water and alteration (e.g., clays, sulfates, carbonates)
- **Products:** Targeted hyperspectral observations and mapping-mode data

## Scientific Value
- Helps identify aqueous alteration minerals and geological history.
- Supports selection and characterization of landing and exploration sites.
- Provides mineralogical context for rover investigations.

## Limitations
- Orbital spectroscopy has spatial and atmospheric limitations; dust can obscure signals.
- Mineral identification is based on spectral interpretation and may require validation.
- Coverage and resolution vary by observing mode.
