# THEMIS (Thermal Emission Imaging System)

## Overview
THEMIS is an instrument on NASA’s 2001 Mars Odyssey orbiter that images Mars in visible and thermal infrared wavelengths to study surface composition and thermal properties.

## Mission and Platform
- **Instrument:** THEMIS
- **Spacecraft:** 2001 Mars Odyssey

## What It Measures
- **Visible imaging:** Surface morphology and context
- **Thermal infrared imaging:** Temperature and thermophysical properties (e.g., thermal inertia), which can indicate rockiness, dust cover, or grain size
- **Compositional clues:** Some mineralogical information from thermal infrared data

## Scientific Value
- Maps surface materials and thermal properties at regional scales.
- Helps identify geologic units and potential landing/exploration areas.
- Supports studies of daily and seasonal temperature variations.

## Limitations
- Thermal properties are influenced by multiple factors (material, grain size, cementation).
- Interpretation requires models and context; not a direct hazard map.
- Resolution and coverage differ from high-resolution visible imagers like HiRISE.

## MARSYN Status
THEMIS data is not currently integrated as a verified scientific layer in MARSYN.
