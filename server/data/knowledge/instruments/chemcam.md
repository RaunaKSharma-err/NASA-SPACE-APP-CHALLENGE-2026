# ChemCam (Chemistry and Camera)

## Overview
ChemCam is an instrument on NASA’s Curiosity rover (Mars Science Laboratory). It uses a laser to vaporize small amounts of rock or soil from a distance and analyzes the resulting plasma to determine elemental composition.

## Mission and Platform
- **Instrument:** ChemCam
- **Rover/Mission:** Curiosity, Mars Science Laboratory (MSL)
- **Location of use:** Gale Crater (rover traverse)

## What It Measures
- **Technique:** Laser-Induced Breakdown Spectroscopy (LIBS)
- **Data type:** Elemental chemistry of targets at standoff distances (typically several meters)
- **Imaging:** Remote Micro-Imager (RMI) provides context images of targets

## Scientific Value
- Enables rapid chemical analysis of rocks and soils without driving to every target.
- Helps identify composition, layering, and alteration relevant to past environments.
- Supports selection of targets for contact science and drilling.

## Limitations
- Provides elemental composition, not full mineral structure by itself.
- Measurements are localized to laser spots and target surfaces (dust/coatings can affect results).
- Findings are specific to sites visited by Curiosity in Gale Crater.

## MARSYN Status
ChemCam measurements are not integrated as datasets in MARSYN. Information here is general instrument/mission knowledge.
