# HiRISE (High Resolution Imaging Science Experiment)

## Overview
HiRISE is a high-resolution camera on NASA’s Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (MRO). It captures detailed images of the Martian surface to study geology, surface processes, and potential landing sites.

## Mission and Platform
- **Instrument:** HiRISE
- **Spacecraft:** Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (MRO)
- **Primary use:** Orbital imaging of Mars

## What It Measures / Observes
- **Data type:** High-resolution visible imagery (and color products in some modes)
- **Typical ground sampling:** Can resolve features down to roughly ~25–30 cm/pixel in optimal conditions (varies with altitude and conditions)
- **Targets:** Craters, layered rocks, dunes, gullies, polar terrain, landing sites, rover tracks

## Scientific Value
- Reveals fine-scale surface features and geological context.
- Helps identify hazards and scientifically interesting targets for landers/rovers.
- Supports studies of active surface processes (e.g., seasonal changes, slope streaks) where observed.

## Limitations
- Imaging coverage is targeted, not continuous global coverage at highest resolution.
- Images are optical observations; composition and subsurface properties require other instruments.
- Interpretation can depend on lighting, season, and atmospheric dust.
