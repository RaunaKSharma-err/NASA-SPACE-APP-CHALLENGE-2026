# SAM (Sample Analysis at Mars)

## Overview
SAM is a laboratory instrument suite on NASA’s Curiosity rover that analyzes gases from the Martian atmosphere and volatile/organic compounds released from rock and soil samples when heated.

## Mission and Platform
- **Instrument:** SAM (Sample Analysis at Mars)
- **Rover/Mission:** Curiosity, Mars Science Laboratory (MSL)
- **Location of use:** Gale Crater

## What It Measures
- **Atmospheric composition:** Gases in the Martian atmosphere
- **Sample analysis:** Volatiles and organic molecules released from drilled or scooped samples
- **Techniques:** Includes gas chromatography, mass spectrometry, and tunable laser spectrometry (instrument suite components)

## Scientific Value
- Characterizes atmospheric chemistry and isotopic ratios relevant to Mars’ climate history.
- Searches for and characterizes organic molecules in rocks/soils.
- Helps assess past environmental conditions and habitability.

## Key Notes
- Detection of organic molecules is scientifically important but does **not** by itself confirm past or present life.
- Results are specific to sampled materials and locations in Gale Crater.

## MARSYN Status
SAM measurements are not integrated as datasets in MARSYN.
