# Spacesuits and Mobility for Mars (Concepts)

## Spacesuit Requirements
Mars surface suits (conceptual/future systems) must address:
- **Pressure:** Maintain breathable, pressurized environment in near-vacuum conditions.
- **Oxygen and life support:** Provide oxygen, CO2 removal, thermal regulation, and humidity control.
- **Mobility:** Allow walking, bending, tool use, and sample handling in ~0.38 g.
- **Dust protection:** Limit dust intrusion and support cleaning/decontamination.
- **Radiation/thermal considerations:** Provide some mitigation and insulation (not complete radiation shielding by suit alone).

## Mobility Considerations
- Terrain includes slopes, rocks, sand, and craters; route choice affects energy use and risk.
- Lower gravity changes gait, balance, and load carrying.
- Rovers, unpressurized vehicles, or pressurized rovers may support longer traverses.
- Navigation must account for communication delays and limited real-time Earth support.

## Planning Factors
- Traverse distance, slope, visibility, suit consumables, time margins, and emergency return paths.
- Surface operations depend on reliable maps/data, but available resolution may be limited.

## MARSYN Context
MARSYN can visualize terrain context and compute slope-based routes from available elevation data. It does not model suit performance, life-support consumables, or operational safety certification.

## Limitations
- Mars EVA suit designs are under development; specifications vary by concept.
