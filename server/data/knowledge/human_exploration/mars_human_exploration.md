# Human Exploration of Mars

## Why Explore Mars with Humans
- Human explorers can perform flexible, adaptive field science, sample selection, and equipment repair in ways that complement robotic missions.
- Mars offers opportunities to study planetary history, climate evolution, geology, and the potential for past life.

## Major Challenges
- **Distance and travel time:** Missions would take months each way depending on trajectory.
- **Life support:** Reliable air, water, food, waste management, and habitat systems are required.
- **Radiation:** Crews would face galactic cosmic rays and solar particle events with limited natural protection.
- **Low gravity:** Mars gravity (~0.38 g) has unknown long-term health effects.
- **Dust and environment:** Dust, low pressure, and temperature extremes affect equipment and operations.
- **Communication delay:** One-way light-time delay can range roughly from ~4 to ~24 minutes depending on planetary positions.

## Current Status
- Human missions to Mars are a long-term goal; no crewed Mars mission has been conducted.
- Robotic missions, technology demonstrations, and analog research inform future planning.

## MARSYN Context
MARSYN is a prototype planning and visualization tool for terrain-aware route exploration and mission context. It is **not** a certified astronaut safety, life-support, or operations system.
