# Mars Environmental Hazards

## Key Hazards for Human and Robotic Operations

### Radiation
- Mars lacks a strong global magnetic field and has a thin atmosphere, so surface radiation exposure is higher than on Earth.
- Sources include galactic cosmic rays and solar particle events.
- Radiation is a major concern for long-duration human missions.

### Low Atmospheric Pressure
- Average surface pressure is ~610 Pa (~0.6% of Earth sea level).
- Humans require pressurized suits/habitats; unprotected exposure is not survivable.

### Atmosphere Composition
- Atmosphere is ~95% CO2 with very little oxygen; not breathable.

### Temperature Extremes
- Temperatures can be extremely cold, with large day/night and seasonal swings.
- Thermal control is critical for crews, electronics, and batteries.

### Dust
- Fine dust can affect seals, mechanisms, optics, solar panels, and human health if inhaled inside habitats.
- Dust storms can reduce visibility and solar power availability.

### Terrain and Mobility
- Slopes, rocks, craters, sand, and rough terrain pose traverse risks.
- Low-resolution terrain data may miss hazards at rover or human scale.

## MARSYN Limitations (important)
- MARSYN’s route planning uses **MOLA elevation/slope** only (within integrated coverage).
- It does **not** model rocks, regolith bearing strength, dust, radiation, weather, or operational safety.
- Route outputs are planning aids, not certified traverse or safety decisions.
