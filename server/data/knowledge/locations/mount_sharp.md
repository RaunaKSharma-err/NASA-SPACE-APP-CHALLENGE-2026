# Mount Sharp (Aeolis Mons)

## Overview
Mount Sharp, formally known as Aeolis Mons, is the central mound inside Gale Crater on Mars. It rises about 5 km above the crater floor in its highest areas and contains extensive layered rock sequences.

## Geological Significance
- The mound’s layers record changes in Mars’ environmental conditions over long periods.
- Lower layers include minerals associated with water (such as clays and sulfates in different strata), suggesting transitions in ancient environments.
- Studying these layers helps scientists reconstruct Gale Crater’s history, including periods when liquid water was present.

## Exploration by Curiosity
- NASA’s Curiosity rover has been ascending Mount Sharp to analyze rock layers in stratigraphic order.
- Measurements of chemistry, mineralogy, and sedimentary structures provide evidence about past habitable conditions.

## MARSYN Context
- MARSYN’s Gale Crater MOLA terrain includes elevation context relevant to Mount Sharp within the integrated bounding box, but at ~463 m/pixel resolution.
- Fine-scale slopes and obstacles on Mount Sharp are not resolved by this DEM.

## Limitations
- Elevation and thickness values vary by reference and location on the mound.
- Interpretations of depositional history are based on available observations and ongoing research.
