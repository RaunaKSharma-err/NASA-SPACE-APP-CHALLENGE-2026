# Gale Crater

## Location and Dimensions
- **Location:** Near the Martian equator in Aeolis region
- **Approximate center:** ~5.4°S, 137.8°E
- **Diameter:** ~154 km
- **Depth/context:** Large impact crater with a prominent central mound

## Key Features
- **Aeolis Mons (Mount Sharp):** Central mound rising several kilometers above parts of the crater floor, with layered sedimentary rocks.
- **Bradbury Landing:** Site where NASA’s Curiosity rover landed on August 6, 2012 (UTC).
- **Crater floor and fans:** Evidence of ancient sediment transport and aqueous processes.

## Scientific Importance
- Selected for Curiosity because it preserves a long record of Martian environmental history in layered rocks.
- Studies have found evidence consistent with ancient habitable environments (liquid water, suitable chemistry, and energy sources for microbial life) in Gale’s past.
- “Habitable environment” does not mean life was found.

## MOLA Topographic Context (MARSYN)
- MARSYN uses verified MOLA MEGDR elevation data for Gale Crater region: **lon 137.0–138.0, lat -5.5 to -4.5**.
- Resolution: **128 pixels/degree (~463 m/pixel at equator)**.
- Elevation units: metres relative to the areoid (per dataset metadata).
- Reported regional context (project metadata): crater floor approximately -4500 m; rim approximately -1000 to 0 m; central mound rises above crater floor (approximate values).

## Limitations
- MARSYN’s integrated terrain coverage is limited to the specified Gale Crater bounding box.
- 463 m resolution is coarse for rover-scale navigation hazards.
